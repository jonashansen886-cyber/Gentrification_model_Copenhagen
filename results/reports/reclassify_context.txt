# Reclassification Reasoning and Context

This document outlines the reasoning used to reclassify Time Series Clusters (TSC) into gentrification levels (1-6). 

## Classification Scale
- **1: High Gentrification**: Strong positive trends in socioeconomic status, rapid influx of gentrifiers, or significant displacement of original lower-income populations.
- **3: Neutral / No Trend**: The cluster follows national averages or shows no significant change over the period.
- **6: Not Gentrifying**: Indicators opposing gentrification (e.g., increasing unemployment, decreasing education levels, or areas remaining stable low-income without investment).

## Variable Logic
The reclassification algorithm evaluates each cluster based on its **mean level** (status) and **slope** (trend). The "direction" indicates whether a higher value (+1) or lower value (-1) correlates with gentrification.

### Demographic Variables
- **age_18_25 (Direction: -1)**: Decreasing proportions often signify the displacement of students or young adults as area prices rise.
- **age_26_40 (Direction: +1)**: The "gentrifier" age group; influx of young professionals is a primary marker.
- **age_41_55 (Direction: +1)**: Correlates with established professionals and families with high purchasing power moving into matured gentrified areas.
- **age_56_69 (Direction: -1)**: Decreasing proportions indicate displacement of older, potentially lower-income long-term residents.
- **EMUB (Single Men without Children) (Direction: +1)**: Singles often lead early-stage gentrification.
- **PMB (Couples with Children) (Direction: -1)**: Often shows a relative decrease in early stages as the area becomes dominated by singles/childless couples.
- **PUB (Couples without Children) (Direction: +1)**: Typical demographic for urban gentrifiers.

### Economic Variables
- **disp_inc (Disposable Income) (Direction: +1)**: Strongest indicator; rising income levels directly track gentrification.
- **unemp (Unemployment) (Direction: -1)**: Decreasing unemployment indicates an influx of workforce-active residents.
- **emp (Employment) (Direction: +1)**: Increasing employment rates follow the transition to a professional-dominated population.
- **ool (Outside Labor Market) (Direction: -1)**: A decrease signifies a move away from marginalized or retiree-heavy demographics.

### Education Variables
- **lvu (Higher Education) (Direction: +1)**: A key marker of the "creative class" and gentrifiers.
- **grund (Primary School Only) (Direction: -1)**: Decrease indicates displacement of lower-skilled residents.
- **gym_erhv (Vocational/Secondary) (Direction: -1)**: Often decreases relative to higher education degrees.

### Housing and Crime
- **mean_price / mean_sqm (Direction: +1)**: Rising property values are a fundamental driver and result of gentrification.
- **public_housing (Direction: -1)**: A decrease in the share of social housing indicates privatization or "upgrading" of the neighborhood.
- **crime_main_y (Direction: -1)**: Reduced crime rates are strongly correlated with urban revitalization and gentrification.

### Migration
- **mig_in (Direction: +1)**: High influx indicates the area is an attractive destination for new (wealthier) residents.
- **mig_net (Direction: +1)**: Positive net migration during growth phases.
- **mig_out (Direction: -1)**: High outflow can signify the displacement of the original population; stability (low rank) is associated with higher values in this classification.

## Methodology
The automated script calculates a normalized score for each cluster within a specific chart. Clusters are then ranked 1 through 6 based on this score, ensuring that the cluster with the strongest signs of gentrification (highest income, lowest unemployment, etc.) is always assigned Value 1.

The gentrification index for each cluster is computed as:

    gent_index = (normalized_mean * 0.5 * direction) + (normalized_slope * 0.5 * direction)

where `normalized_mean` and `normalized_slope` are min-max scaled within each chart. Mean and slope are weighted equally (50/50). The final rank is linearly mapped from the sorted index onto the 1–6 scale.

## TSC Structure
Time Series Clustering is run **per 10-year interval** (not over the full 30-year period). This produces 3 period-specific `.gpkg` files and HTML charts per variable:
- 1990–2000, 2000–2010, 2010–2020

Total: 21 variables × 3 periods = 63 charts and GeoPackages. Each chart is reclassified independently, so cluster assignments are not comparable across periods — only the gentrification rank (1–6) is.

Source GeoPackages: `data/processed/tsc_netcdf_vector/{var}_{start}_{end}_tsc.gpkg`
Source charts: `results/metrics/charts_html/{var}_{start}_{end}_tsc_timeseries.html`
Output: `results/reports/reclassified.txt`

Note: Older 5-year period HTML charts (1990–1995, …, 2015–2020) remain in `charts_html/` for reference. The `reclassified.txt` contains entries for all periods present in that folder; only the 10-year period entries are consumed by the active pipeline (Notebook 02 onwards).
